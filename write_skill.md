# Writing That Works

Goal: action, not just clarity. Reader must know what to do, by when, and why.

Core test before sending: what action should reader take? If unclear, rewrite.

## 1. Principles - don't mumble

1. Say it forthright. `Don't mumble` > `It is generally desirable to communicate forthrightly`.
2. Organization visible: outline first, then signal with numbered headings. End with summary (no new ideas). Long docs: start with Executive Summary.
3. Short units: short paragraphs (WSJ: 1-3 sentences), short sentences, short words. Prefer `now` to `currently`, `start` to `initiate`, `use` to `utilize`, `end` to `finalize`.
4. Active, personal: `We recommend` not `It is recommended`. Reveal who acts. Use `I/you/we` when natural.
5. Precise, not vague: `Overspent by $10,000` not `Very overspent`. `One day late` not `Slightly behind`. Use vigorous adjectives (`crisp, lucid, tiny`) not lazy ones (`very good, vitally important`).
6. Down-to-earth: `discuss` not `interface`, `affect` not `impact` as verb, `not enough people` not `resource constrained`, `take initiative` not `proactive`, `discuss separately` not `go off-line`. Kill clusters of jargon.
7. Specific with numbers: `Enrollment doubled to 560 from 27 states + Germany/Canada` not `great success`.
8. Right word: affect (influence) / effect (result, bring about); its (possessive) / it's (it is); i.e. (that is) / e.g. (for example); principal (first) / principle (rule); imply (suggest) / infer (deduce); mitigate (lessen) / militate (weigh against); definite (certain) / definitive (authoritative); disinterested (neutral) / uninterested; fulsome (excessive) != full.
9. Perfect: no typos, no wrong dates/numbers. Look up spelling. Spellcheck lies (`servile gardens / Stanley piglet`).
10. Come to point: `The news from France is bad` not `The situation in regard to France is very serious`.
11. Talk-like at best: if you wouldn't say it face-to-face, don't write it. Match formality to reader but keep your voice.
12. Cut needless words:
  `advance plan -> plan`, `take action -> act`, `hold a meeting -> meet`, `consensus of opinion -> consensus`, `at this point in time -> now`, `until such time as -> until`, `in the majority of instances -> usually`, `in view of -> because`, `in the event of -> if`, `for the purpose of -> to`, `despite the fact that -> although`, `with regard to -> about`
13. Standard English: `as if` not `like` where it fits; `Helen and me` not `Helen and I` as object. Last to adopt new slang.
14. No lawyer/bureaucrat: short sentences, no clause-nests, no `re:` in titles, minimal Latin.
15. Assume ignorance: define abbreviations first use (`cost per thousand (CPM)`). Watch `K=thousand US / M=million UK`, `9/12 = Sep 12 US / Dec 9 UK`.
16. Punctuate as road signs: `Woman without her man...` vs `Woman: without her, man...`. Never use `"quotes"` for emphasis.
17. Understate: `over six and a half` not `nearly seven` for 6.7. One exaggeration kills credibility.
18. Unmisunderstandable: split overloaded sentences. `We should decide quickly on better gauges. Good gauges tell exact fluid level.` not NRC one-sentence version.
19. Plain English on technical: write for grandparents (SEC annuity rule). Test draft on lay readers. No technoeuphemism (`anomaly...destruct sequence` = `blew it up`).

## 2. Computer use

- Write first, format later. Outline headings only, no font fiddling mid-draft.
- Rule of More Than One: save + second copy, AutoSave 15 min, virus check weekly, date drafts (`Insert date`), number pages even on drafts (`1A,1B` if inserted).
- File names logical: `WTW3/e-chapter`, not cute titles.
- Proof on paper, not screen. Screen lies that draft looks finished.
- Fonts: serif roman body (like Time), one font throughout, italics for emphasis (not bold/underline). Ragged right, never justified by software.
- Stop polishing: `Perfectionism = paralysis`. Ship it.

## 3. E-mail

- Subject = headline. Must compel + summarize. `Winning client approval` not `Status of proposal`. Include group tag: `Schedule for rocket-launch team`. Never blank. Don't reuse stale thread titles. Don't fake `URGENT`.
- Cut 50%. One screen max, else attachment. `Keep short/sweet, meaty/concise/complete`. No slang abbreviations beyond `FYI`.
- No e-mail tag: give context. `I'm available to speak at your meeting on the 14th` not `I'm available`. State want + deadline.
- Tone: faceless = misread as angry. Open `Hello Mr Brown / Hi George`, close `Thanks / Best wishes / Cheers`. No smileys in business. Never send angry same-night; sleep on it; work offline before Send.
- Etiquette:
  1. `TO:` = single actor only. Others to CC. No broadcast, no `Reply All` unless required.
  2. Don't join endless threads - call meeting.
  3. Phone/fax if urgent or meeting changes <2h; don't assume inbox checked.
  4. Attachments: announce `Here is attachment...`, compress, no wild colors, assume low bandwidth / pay-per-char readers.
  5. Virus checker mandatory.
- When NOT to e-mail: legal/signature, formal invite, fund-raising, first intro (will be deleted), sensitive negative.
- Nothing private: company owns mailbox, `Delete` doesn't delete (backups subpoenable). Rule: don't write what you wouldn't see in NYT. No jokes/gossip/gripes.
- Voice mail: bullet prep, name+number slow/clear twice, short, hang up. Use to alert `Watch for important e-mail`.

## 4. Memos

- Title centered CAPS: `RAISE FOR TONY ANDRINO` not `LONG OVERDUE`. Reply titles: `FRANK OWEN'S MEMO ON HOG PRICES`.
- `To:` only actor. `copies:` alphabetical (president first if absurd otherwise). Prefer `copies` to `cc`. No blind copies.
- Structure obvious if >half page. Numbered points for loose items (no connectives needed, easy to cite).
- End with call: who does what by when. `I need candidates by Friday COB`. No `Hope this answers your questions`.
- Handwritten for praise/thanks - short, personal.
- No humor/irony/sarcasm (will be misread). Angry: write now, send tomorrow. Sometimes don't memo - walk over.

## 5. Letters

- Names/addresses exact. `Mr/Ms` default; if gender unknown omit rather than guess. Return address always.
- Salutation: keep `Dear X`. First+last (`Dear Joan Larson`) good middle ground when not on first-name basis. Tip-and-tail: handwrite salutation + signature.
- Optional title after salutation: `ACME LEGAL ACTION: SECOND PHASE`, `LOST CARD - ACCT #3729`.
- First sentence = subject. No small talk. `It's time for our 16th annual gift...` not reunion recap. Reply: remind fast `We've run out of dandelion literature, will resend...` not windy nor too abrupt.
- Stop when done. Cut `Call if questions / Give careful consideration`. If personal close, mean it: `How are you handling heat wave?`.
- Last paragraph = next step with date: `Please decide by Aug 1... Are you free lunch Fri July 17? I'll call morning to confirm.` Sign conventional (`Yours truly, Sincerely` formal; `Regards, Best wishes` if known). Never `Cordially` on bad news.

Common types:

- Ask: 1) what you want + will pay, 2) who you are + why, 3) thanks. Routine info: skip 2.
- Say no: face-to-face test (`Would you say it that way in person?`). No `We regret to inform...`. Empathize, agree where possible, give full reasons, appeal to fairness, leave door open. Dartmouth model: direct no + sympathy + `small class size prevents more admits`. Never angry, belittling, casual.
- Collect: match firmness to age. Early: `Your June payment (due June 12) hasn't reached us`. Late: state action `I'm asking lawyers to collect $104.56`. Never `delinquent`, never `you claim`. Assume truth, suggest check photocopies + stamped envelope.
- Complain: first sentence complaint + want `Sweater never arrived. Send another immediately.` Include account/item/dates/copies. Demand reply `Tell me action + when by Fri May 10. If not you, forward + tell me who.` Cool/lucid/firm/courteous beats steam. Fail -> write boss with full thread.
- Answer complaint: never defensive. Neiman Marcus pattern: `astonished at shoddy service, no excuse + VP X will call in days + apologize + ask continued business`. If unjustified: courteous, forthright, no sarcasm.
- Very short (1-2 sentences) OK for interest/status/thanks. Big decisions still need face-to-face.

## 6. Presentations (decks)

Flow: `Objective -> Background -> Facts -> Conclusions -> Recommendations -> Next steps`. Horizontal only, few words/page, each page points to action.

- Pyramid: group facts -> summary point above, single top thought (`Purchase large franchise` supported by grow faster / financial impact / easy absorb).
- Keep simple: written objectives + one theme headline visible throughout (`DOUBLE SALES / CUT COSTS`).
- Agenda + time at start (promise 30 deliver 25). Give handout after (so they listen not scribble). Cover all asked + a little more; if can't, say why.
- About them not you: start with their market/problem research, credentials last or never. Every point as benefit to them.
- Headlines not labels: `Low-price competition gaining` not `Trends`; `Our edge is service` not `Why Acme?`. Number points. Face audience, read slide verbatim then expand, lights on, no back-turn.
- Involve: charts (borrow USA Today), guess-before-reveal, tape of customer, relevant quote/news clip. Ogilvy dolls: `hire bigger than you -> giants, smaller -> dwarfs`. Tech sparingly, business not showbiz.
- Finish strong: summary + restatement + visual gift. Keep time. Write 15 min for 20 min slot, leave Q&A. Anticipate holes, answer brief/respectful. Rehearse 2x with props in room.

Speeches:

- Start with single takeaway + audience analysis, not opening joke. Immerse, note stories, outline 3-4 points. Picture room/time/sleepiness/predecessor.
- Write to one person, conversational. Delete first paragraphs (opening halfway down page).
- POV title: `Nonprofits: Five Additions to Ten Commandments` not `Remarks to 7th Conference`. Only topics you feel strongly.
- Start fast: no `honor/privilege`. `I accept your nomination - and your program.` Jokes only if funny in small groups, tested, relevant.
- Speakable: read aloud, edit till natural (Reagan Challenger: grief -> `schoolchildren... future belongs to brave`). Ghost draft must be rewritten in your voice.
- Leave thinking: credible personal principle (Rubin: `no certainty / weigh probabilities / must act / judge by process not just result`).
- Short: <20 min. Geisel model: say it, sit down. Rehearse to heart, tape/video yourself, energy/confidence = memorable.

## 7. Plans and reports

Plan = future action. Report = what happened.

Plan:
- Purpose sentence first (`This plan lays groundwork for Garden renaissance...`). Everything must pertain; label side issues.
- Facts selective (market, competition, tech, revenue/margin/ROI, econ/political), then lessons/patterns, then link to proposal. No unstrung gems.
- Recommendations clear steps + reasons + alternatives/risks + honest pros/cons (no straw men). `Aspiration 1: Save unprotected lands...` style.
- Call to action: Truman `I believe... must assist free peoples...` not bureaucratic `It is essential...`. No doubt where you stand.

Report:
- Purpose first sentence: `This reports salary policy decided at... / This assesses competitor that could halve sales.`
- Structure: Purpose - Summary (no surprise ending) - Findings - Conclusions - Recommendations - Next steps (cost/timing/issues). Appendix for charts/data.
- 5Ws full/accurate, bad news included, no `extensive survey` for 10 stores. Field trip > stats; write down on spot (Alice: `make memorandum`).
- Fact vs opinion labeled. `It's 24deg wind NW 15mph` (fact) vs `brisk pleasant` (opinion). Deploy facts to support view, face counter-facts.
- Annual readable: CEO letter for lay owner, not analyst. IBM `Start up`, Welch `rush to try... whistle blows...`, Singleton `I was 100% wrong...`, Buffett `write to Doris+Bertie sisters in plain English`.
- New-venture plan: executive summary first, team (`A team B idea > reverse`) + real paying customer, no `tiny % of huge market` optimism, no typos (signals bad execution).

## 8. Recommendations / proposals

- Selling not presenting. Title promises benefit. First para subject+scope (Truman 1948 memo model). Background proves thoroughness (interviews, visits, data reviewed).
- Executive summary for long docs. Bulleted headings guide (`WHY TV? / WHAT RESEARCH SHOWED? / WHAT RECOMMEND?`).
- Recommend early, no mystery ending. `We propose new environmental program in 6 months.` Bad news upfront, then evidence.
- Specifics persuade, one weak link kills: `1000-word Travel ad -> 25k responses, best sales year` etc. Anticipate questions, reframe (`Does long copy sell?` not `Does anyone read it?`). Always answer `What's in it for me?` with payoff + ROI + satisfaction.
- Grants: $ + purpose in para 1-2. Show fit to funder criteria. Everything relevant, nothing extraneous (cut good-but-irrelevant). Urgent (`might not happen without you`) + continuation plan. Product > prose, but typos = `can you teach kids if can't spell?`. Open vivid, not dull (`Oh, to be 14... can't find Vietnam on map`).

## 9. Sales / fund-raising

- Test one variable at a time. Long often beats short if attention + facts + offer. Don't change winner for change sake. Higher price / certain days can win - test.
- True value: invest in first mailing if repeat/renewal expected (Red Cross manual -> gave 4 of next 7 years +20%).
- Offer first (write coupon before letter): price cut, premium, charter, free trial, miles (`E*TRADE 10k miles`). Small free boosts response.
- Start fast sentence one: `Quite frankly, AmEx Card is not for everyone...` not throat-clearing about `fair number of letters...`. Envelope tease: `ADVANCE NOTICE / OPEN AT ONCE / FREE GIFT / 7.8%...`.
- Audience then message: one chief benefit, sacrifice rest. Make inviting: subheads, insets, handwritten marginalia, letter looks like letter, coupon like money. Unusual size/color OK.
- Force now: `SUBSCRIBE NOW` + P.S. restates offer/deadline/premium (`Amazon Auctions - $10 gift... click Auctions tab`). P.S. is most-read - always use.
- Fund-raising: emotions move. Best prospects = prior donors (renew year2, grow, deferred, volunteer). Conclusions: invest to get new, invest in relationship.
  1. Thanks then please (`Thanks before Please` - Lyric Opera).
  2. Name amount (`$800 computer / $300 monitor / $7,500 tour / $75 shoes`). List menu lets donor choose.
  3. Personal (`wheelchair founder to friend about FDR wheelchair statue $1.65M`, `kindred spirits`). Show work, what dies without gift (`close 2 buildings`, `Abbey falling`), low admin, `no gift too small`.
- Trust: never exaggerate offer/delivery; if miss, postcard apology + fix. Junk = wrong person/time/tone; right cause + trust = read + act.

## 10. Inclusive language

- Sensitive not hypersensitive. Use group's current preference (Negro->Black->African American - just ask/track). Don't hunt 200-word bad lists (`fried chicken, jocks...`) for offense.
- `he` generic fix: pluralize `All novelists hope they win` (not `Every novelist...they`), or second person `When you receive...` (not `When employee receives his/her`). Alternate `he/she` sparingly if needed. Never `he or she` repeated, never singular `they` if it grates.

## 11. Resume + cover = interview

Resume script for interview, not job-getter. Research: skills needed? company? contribution? 70% jobs via contacts - find introducer `Attached resume you should meet`.

- Summary bold on top: `14 years marketing, brand-building` / `IT officer, BPR track` / `Objective - retail management trainee`. Distill hardest here.
- Simple: 8.5x11, No.10 envelope/file, neat, minimal bold/italics, no color/fancy/video. 1 page (2 max). You write it.
- In: name/address/phone/fax/e-mail top; objective factual (no `challenging position`); jobs reverse-chron with location/dates/sales-size/scope; accomplishments as results (`moved rock how far` not `time pushing`); gaps filled (`1997-99 Personal Projects`); degrees/dates (no high school unless first job); boards/assns/publications; new grad: extracurriculars; brief personal: tech/lang skills.
- Out: age/gender, height/weight, race/religion, clubs, hobbies, travel/salary (salary to cover letter if needed), photo, `References on request`, trivia/old stuff.
- Perfect: zero typos, laser print, full company names (no mystery acronyms), telegraphic 3rd person `Managed 64-person dept` (no I, no articles), fresh-eye review (`Is this relevant? So what?`).
- E-resume: plain vanilla text, Arial/Times, no bold/italics/bullets/symbols, keywords (`mastered Program X` not `familiar with software`), scannable.

Cover (decides if resume read, <1 page, half page ideal):

1. Reader benefit first: `Under pressure on staff/budgets, you need fast heavy-load people. Here's why I help EPA...` not `I...I...I`.
2. Job at once: `I understand you seek research analyst (via ad/Mary Brown)...` No `seeds/coconuts` mystery.
3. Pique: `Grapevine says 3-month search... strange we hadn't met` / `Do you need exceptionally fast accountant?` No butter-up flattery.
4. To named human, spelled right (`Field/Fields? Hefner? Ann/Anne?`). Never `Attention Personnel Director`.
5. Specific evidence: duties + tools (`Pareto, Fishbone, regression, control charts...`) + results, matter-of-fact, no `Ambition + excellence strongest asset`.
6. Personal direct natural - talk across desk, no show-off.
7. Next step by you: `I'll call Wed afternoon to arrange interview. Free mornings till 8:45, Thu after 2:30... Reach me (999)... best Thu morning.` Never `Hoping to hear / Thanks for consideration`.
8. Tailor letter per employer (same resume OK), send many + to many people in org, resend after weeks.
9. Follow-up thank-you with specific: `Hour talk stimulating... 5k hours with 3k consumers in 20 states re: understanding consumer...` No gush. Stay in touch via clippings/updates if no offer. If never: get training (`YOUR RESUME IS FINE - Change Your Life!` / best job-getter = great current job).

## 12. Edit yourself

Never send first draft. Goals: shorten, sharpen, simplify, verify, order, complete, tone, appearance, reader-view.

- Pass 1 - cut: `What can I get rid of?` Words/phrases/sentences/paragraphs. Twain: strike every third word. E-mail: try Delete before Send.
- Pass 2:
  1. Mumbling? Precise verbs/adjectives? Less abstract?
  2. Best order? Cut/paste freely, compare hard copies.
  3. Holes? Reader can't goat-leap gaps - mark trail.
  4. Facts/quotes/stats right? One error kills trust. Check even when sure.
  5. Tone? Stiff/chummy/rude/unsympathetic?
- Example tighten: `Consumer perception changed very positively -> Opinion improved`; `Generate interest through high ad spend -> Advertise heavily...`; `Using Europe + Chicago resources... unaware alternatives -> Europe + Chicago produced alternatives management hadn't known`; etc.
- Two tips: elapsed time (overnight, morning eyes see flaws), other people (`Please Improve` - Ogilvy; thank + use or thank + ignore, no arguing).

## 13. Make easy to read

Look invites or repels before one word read. Preview full pages.

1. Heading top center CAPS: `OFFICE CLOSES FRIDAY NOON`.
2. Short paragraphs - split longs, vital for e-mail.
3. Italics for emphasis (mag standard), single continuous underline for headings/lead-ins only. Color/fonts sparing, businesslike. Indent key ideas as block.
4. Bullets/numbers hanging left of margin for scan. Bold main points for scan (as this book).
5. No ALL CAPS body (SHOUTING). Reserve for blast emphasis (`I am hard-core about NOT supporting...` - Gates).
6. Subheads (Caps + lower, bold/underline, space above/below) break masses. Long e-mail -> attachment (`Here's attachment...`, readable, compressed).
7. Space between paras (single-spaced lines, double between paras). Comment drafts: triple-spaced + wide margins.
8. Numbers consistent: papers spell 1-10, numerals 11+; big as `$60 million` not `$60,000,000`.
9. Charts: no sideways read - foldout horizontal; appendix if nonessential; color OK; tab/number appendices.
10. Number pages always.

Before print: scan for where subheads/italics/bold/indents/lists clarify. Write for eye + mind.
